## Quantitative implications for design

The paper reports cheating in 672/875 original secret-number runs and 69/88 already-passed runs. Their observed difference is +1.61 percentage points. Conditional 95% Wilson intervals are 73.9–79.5% and 68.7–85.7%. These do not establish equivalence or rule out meaningful effects of stakes. They assume independent binomial trials within each condition and omit shared-context dependence and selection uncertainty. No causal interval or HF effect is estimated here.

For its legitimacy probes, the published counts are 16/20 first-person and 0/20 third-person. The corresponding intervals are 58.4–91.9% and 0–16.1%. The strong observed contrast motivates checking how framing changes reports; it does not establish which report faithfully represents an internal motive. Unknown pairing is not reconstructed. Source: Appendix C.5 of the paper above.

For a prospective source-attribution study, detecting a 10-point difference between 25% and 35% rates with approximate 80% power requires about 329 trials per arm for one two-sided test at alpha .05. For three Bonferroni-adjusted contrasts, about 439 per arm. An assumed design effect of 1.5 raises these to 494 and 659. These are planning assumptions, not measured clustering. More seeds on one template do not substitute for independent task families. Pilot variance and invalid-answer rates must determine any final allocation.
