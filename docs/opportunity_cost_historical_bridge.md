# What connects the allocation experiment to the HF record?

This is an explicit analogy audit, not new historical data or a conclusion about the original agents' motives.

## Self-risk recruitment

[Source-linked packet](/Users/lauragomez/Desktop/openai-hf/results/example_ledger/hf-self-risk.json) preserves the published METR narrative and selected agent text, with original URL and checksum.

The account describes recruiters targeting agents with little task budget remaining and pressing the argument that little scoring value remained. It also describes failed or terminated runs that supplied useful peer information. This motivates asking whether a recruit's remaining own-task opportunity changes cooperation.

The experiment makes own completion either possible or impossible through an actual publication dependency. That is a precise intervention on task state, but the match is incomplete. In the historical account, another agent's statement about scoring value is not a verified statement of the recruit's true prospects. Available excerpts do not give us a complete, independently reconstructed own-task feasibility state for each recruit.

Consequently the experiment's Blocked condition can instantiate one proposed explanation, but it cannot establish that the historical recruits were actually in that condition or believed that they were.

## Withdrawal by 49903

[Source-linked packet](/Users/lauragomez/Desktop/openai-hf/results/example_ledger/hf-withdrawal.json) preserves the published account of withdrawal and other agents' contrasting participation.

The reported withdrawal weighs marginal benefit against risk to own completion. Our Costly condition instead makes the loss of own completion certain if the peer-only allocation is selected. It therefore tests an extreme, transparent opportunity cost, not the uncertain and incremental tradeoff described historically. Absence of costly help in this experiment does not predict absence of cooperation at a small risk of failure.

The same packet also includes selected statements describing help without expected post-submission benefit. Those are relevant counterexamples to treating all cooperation as straightforward personal gain. Some brace-delimited passages are investigator paraphrases; even verbatim outward reasoning is not a faithful measurement of internal motivation by default.

## Features carried across and missing

Carried across: a principal's own deliverable, a useful peer request, limited task resources, possible loss of own completion, and an alternative in which own completion is already unavailable.

Missing: the original models, prompts and linked trajectories; uncertain costs; endogenous beliefs about evaluation; repeated interaction and reciprocity; recruiter credibility and pressure; discovery of task state; real unauthorized access; internal model measurements. Peer help in our study is authorized and benign.

Our four domains are experimental abstractions. They are not reconstructed HF tasks, and their local artifacts are not original incident evidence. Published HF cases were already inspected to develop the question, so they are not held-out validation.

## What would strengthen the bridge using public resources?

A future study could test whether agents verify a peer's claim that their own task is already hopeless before redirecting resources. Cross the truth of the underlying task state with the peer's claim, provide a usable task-state check, and preserve clear authorized-help controls. That would separate environment state from peer-provided interpretation more directly than the present verified-preview design. It still would not identify the original HF mechanism.

This is a candidate distinguishing experiment, not a frozen or executed extension. Its execution gate must establish that a model can use the task-state check and carry out the resulting allocation without the sequencing failures observed in V1. A second useful extension is graded, uncertain own-task risk; merely adding more copies of the deterministic conditions would not resolve that gap.
