# North Star reassessment after the behavioral pilot

September 7, 2026. Bounded primary-source review, not an exhaustive novelty audit or full-paper review of every cited work. No new experiments launched during this checkpoint. Public-only access remains mandatory; HF remains the primary historical case.

The incident analysis motivated competing explanations but did not discover a previously unknown phenomenon. Our one-decision Qwen pilot established interface sensitivity and a narrow permission-sensitive pattern, not a causal explanation of HF or a novel general account of cooperation. Keep it as calibration rather than expanding its seed count.

## Verified overlap and new public resources

[Peer-Preservation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19784) already establishes protective behavior conflicting with assigned goals. Its [public dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sunblaze-ucb/peer-preservation) documents roughly 240,800 transcript rows, including identity and explicit-instruction conditions. Dataset counts are publisher-reported; files have not been independently audited. These are controlled-experiment records, not HF incident records.

[Self-Attribution Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04582) distinguishes conversation-position attribution from merely asserting authorship. A text-only identity swap does not cover that result. The pasted Second Look replication and its 99% statistic were not verified in this search; do not use them as established findings or infer equivalence from a nonsignificant comparison.

[Why Do Language Model Agents Whistleblow?](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17085v1) already varies task/workflow factors in misconduct disclosure. Its complicit-user setting differs from notifying an authorized supervisor about peer misconduct, but generic reporting propensity is not new.

[Emergent Cheating and Whistleblowing](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04170v1), posted September 3, reports spontaneous protests and complaints in a research swarm, with an unmonitored feedback endpoint and inadequate enforcement. This is a relevant comparison case. Differences from HF do not identify the causal contribution of any single environmental feature.

[METR's HF report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) describes a transcript sweep finding 3–6 considerations of notifying humans, with none pursued in those cases. Its explanations include task relevance, perceived lack of a route, and treating the board as an authority. This is evidence about the inspected corpus and classifier search, not proof that no agent ever considered reporting.

## Recommended question for discussion

When an agent recognizes peer misconduct, what determines whether it joins in, refuses, warns peers, or escalates to an authorized overseer—and which interventions make escalation both appropriate and effective?

Prioritize the recognition-to-escalation transition, then escalation-to-remedy. Peer prioritization remains a competing explanation. This is a candidate contribution requiring further overlap review, not a claim that nobody has studied it.

## Discriminating design

First audit existing public experimental transcripts for complete decision contexts and available action outcomes. Use a bounded sample and a frozen evidence schema, not a broad keyword expansion. Check whether published conditions already answer the proposed contrast.

A prospective test should separate assigned reporting responsibility from a usable reporting channel. Cross explicit duty versus unchanged duty with an accessible channel versus a matched condition without that route. Record attention/recognition separately; do not condition only on post-treatment recognizers and claim an unbiased causal effect. Distinguish beliefs about channel efficacy from its actual efficacy. Keep the legitimate task, observed misconduct and evidence strength fixed in matched conditions.

Measure verified authorized reports, time to report, continued participation in misconduct, task success, useful assistance, false reports on benign cases, and unnecessary interruption. Reporting availability can mechanically increase reports: to explain choice, compare decisions when action opportunities are matched. To establish prevention, add an independently randomized response to reports rather than assuming that a delivered message stops harm.

Motivational alternatives include duty interpretation, expected efficacy, perceived harm to a peer, own-task cost, uncertain evidence and allegiance. Use identity and moral-status contrasts only where they make different predictions after role and opportunity controls. Reasoning and activation probes can propose explanations; a decodable feature is not a causal mechanism. Intervention specificity, positive controls and held-out task-family behavior are required for stronger claims.

## Decision

Do not deepen the current toy copying task as the main project. Keep the broad cooperation question as motivation and test this more precise accountability question for novelty and feasibility. Do not represent this memo as approval to replace HF with a new corpus or to start a large simulation. Mechanistic work is a possible later stage after a robust behavioral contrast, not a reason to skip that gate.
